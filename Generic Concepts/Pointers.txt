What is a pointer?

When I think of the word 'pointer' the first image that pops into my head is that of a person pointing to something, and that is actually a
pretty solid (no one hears....) representation of the concept.

In really simple terms, a pointer is a variable which stores an address in memory. That's about it. There is nothing more to them.
Sorry if you were expecting some really complicated explanation.

But I suppose this definition is not enough to inspire some ideas in the minds of the one reading, as to why pointers even exist? I mean what
is their purpose in a program?

On the contrary I would hate to program in a language where the concept of pointers doesn't exist. You see, the fact that a pointer stores an address
is really powerful. Because the address could lead to anything, it could lead to another pointer, a normal variable, a function, an array, an object, literally
anything. Because everything at the end of the day is stored in memory (RAM) and if you know the address of whatever it is you want to access
you can do so pretty easily using pointers.

The actual use cases of pointers are numerous and intricate so I won't bore you with the details, but you should never look down upon them,
they are really powerful.
